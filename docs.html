Technical notes on the products

How each product on this site is made: data sources, model grids, runs used, methods, update times and archives. Prepared by Krishna Mishra, Scientist D, RWFC New Delhi, India Meteorological Department. These are guidance products, not official IMD forecasts.

2. Times, runs and categories

  • IC (initial condition) is the model run time, e.g. 00 UTC 03 Nov. IST = UTC + 5:30.
  • Runs used: the district rainfall products use only the 00 and 12 UTC runs of every model, so all models refer to the same 24-hour windows. The cyclone MME uses each model's latest available run.
  • Day n on the forecast tabs is the n-th 24 hours after the IC: 00 to 00 UTC (05:30 to 05:30 IST) for a 00 UTC run, 12 to 12 UTC (17:30 to 17:30 IST) for a 12 UTC run.
  • Verification day follows IMD's rainfall day: 03 UTC to 03 UTC (08:30 to 08:30 IST).
  • Late runs: a model or member whose new run is not yet published is shown from its previous run and labelled with that run, e.g. NCUM-R (28/12Z) or ECMWF IFS (12Z).

3. T-phi grams and station indices (IMD RS/RW data) ↑ top

Data

  • Source: IMD CLIMS RS/RW data for all stations. For each ascent the File-3 standard summary (_sum.txt) is read, or the File-4 zip when File-3 is missing.
  • Levels used: time, pressure (hPa), temperature, relative humidity, dew point, geopotential height, wind direction and speed.
  • About 45 stations report at a typical 00 UTC ascent.

What is drawn

  • T-phi gram with the parcel path; positive area (CAPE) shaded red and negative area (CIN) blue; wind barbs.
  • An index sheet of 39 values per station, including: surface-based, mixed-layer and most-unstable CAPE; CIN; LCL, LFC and EL; lifted index and Showalter index; K index; total totals; SWEAT; precipitable water; DCAPE and the corresponding maximum downdraft speed; bulk wind shear and storm-relative helicity; supercell composite (SCP) and significant tornado parameter (STP); wet-bulb zero height.
  • Quality flags: values outside physical limits (e.g. CAPE above 7000 J/kg, precipitable water outside 2–90 mm, K index outside −40 to 60) are flagged on the sheet, not hidden. Check the ascent before using a flagged index.

Station Indices (7 days) tab

  • Every value on the index sheet is also saved for each station and ascent. The tab draws them as one small chart per index for the last 7 days, grouped as convective energy, moisture, stability indices, heights and levels, boundary layer and inversion, and shear and severe weather.
  • 00 UTC and 12 UTC are drawn as two separate lines, so the diurnal difference does not hide the trend. A gap of more than 30 hours breaks the line.
  • A red ring marks an ascent that carried a data-check flag on its T-phi gram; check that chart before using the value.
  • Choose the station from the list (or step with ◀ ▶). The page opens on New Delhi and then remembers the last station. A link can open one station directly, e.g. #delhi-42182 (New Delhi) or #delhi-42809 (Kolkata).
  • The last 10 days are kept for every station; the 7-day window ends on the newest ascent of any station.

Updates and archive

07:30 and 09:00 IST for 00 UTC ascents; 19:30 and 21:00 IST for 12 UTC. The later run adds stations that report late. A rolling archive keeps all ascents; view by ascent or by station and filter by name.

Own tracker (gridded models)

  • Fields are cut to 0–35°N, 40–100°E, every 6 h up to +240 h.
  • Centre: minimum of MSLP smoothed over about 50 km, searched within 400 km of the starting position, then within 250 km of the position extrapolated from the last 6 hours.
  • A centre is accepted only if it is at least 1 hPa deeper than the mean MSLP in a 300–500 km ring (2.5 hPa over land); tracking stops after a jump of more than 300 km in 6 h, or when a decaying low over land becomes too weak.
  • MSW = highest 10 m wind within 300 km of the centre; central pressure = lowest MSLP within 75 km.
  • Landfall = first ocean-to-land crossing of the track, found on the coastline by bisection and interpolated in time; the nearest coastal place is named.
  • The starting position of the system in each basin comes from ECMWF's tracker for that basin.

Two basins: the basin selector

  • At every update, the IMD MME and the ECMWF ensemble are run separately for the Arabian Sea and for the Bay of Bengal, so a system in each basin gets its own tracks, plumes and tables, even when both basins are active at the same time.
  • On the IMD MME and ECMWF ENS tabs, the drop-down next to the run chooses the basin. Each option shows how many maps that basin has in the selected run, e.g. “Bay of Bengal · 7 maps”, so an active basin is easy to spot.
  • When a basin has no system in a run, it shows a single “no system” card (“No system tracked over this basin in this run”), not an error. The basin is checked again at every update, and the maps replace the card as soon as a system appears.
  • The page opens on the Arabian Sea; the basin you choose is kept while you move between runs and tabs, until the browser tab is closed.
  • Download links are labelled with the basin, and the ECMWF run-comparison table is made for each basin separately.
  • The IMD model files (IMD GFS, NCUM-G, NCUM-R) are downloaded once per run and used for both basins.
  • The A-Deck tab has no selector: the a-deck of each numbered system is shown with its basin in the title.

MME

  • Every 6 hours, the simple mean of latitude, longitude, MSW and MSLP of the members present at that time (at least 2 members).
  • A model whose run is not out is used from its earlier run (up to 24 h older), from the matching time, and labelled with that run.
  • Written as MMM_track.txt in IMD's MME text format.

Products, updates and archive

Combined track/MSW/MSLP figure, model tracks, MSW and MSLP of all models, MME track and MSW, MME MSLP with a 6-hourly table, landfall table. Six updates a day: 10:30, 13:30, 14:30, 22:30, 01:30 and 02:30 IST (05, 08, 09, 17, 20, 21 UTC). Last 15 days (30 runs) kept.

5. ECMWF ensemble cyclone tracks ↑ top

  • Data: ECMWF's tropical-cyclone track files (BUFR) from ECMWF open data, for the ensemble (50 perturbed members + control) and HRES. Resolution of the IFS ensemble: ~9 km.
  • For each member, the track of the system is the basin track passing closest to the reference position (within 4°) during the first 72 h.
  • Ensemble mean track, MSW and MSLP: mean of the members tracking the system at each time, shown where at least 10 members remain. Plumes show the 10–90 percentile band.
  • Strike probability: share of the 51 tracks whose centre passes within 120 km of a point at any time in the forecast (tracks interpolated hourly).
  • Run comparison: the last six runs of the selected basin side by side (peak, landfall time and place, members making landfall), also saved as a PNG for each basin.
  • Run separately for the Arabian Sea and the Bay of Bengal; choose the basin with the drop-down (see the basin selector).
  • New runs appear at about 13:30 IST (00 UTC run) and 01:30 IST (12 UTC run). Last 15 days kept.

6. A-Deck: JTWC model tracks ↑ top

  • Source: the open a-deck files of the UCAR RAL Tropical Cyclone Guidance Project. An a-deck exists only after JTWC numbers the system (e.g. 02B, file aio02YYYY.dat).
  • Plotted: NAVGEM (NGX), UKMO (UKM), CMC, and the GEFS (AEMN), Canadian (CEMN) and Navy (NEMN) ensemble means, each with its own centre's tracker.
  • Made from each basin's MME run, so systems in both basins appear, each titled with its basin.
  • Run used: the latest a-deck run with at least three deterministic models. NAVGEM from here is also an MME member; the others are for comparison only.
  • Updated with every MME update; last 15 days kept; track files downloadable.

District value

  • For each model, district and day: the highest grid-point rainfall inside the district (742 districts). The grid point nearest the district centre is always included, so very small districts also get a value.
  • A district maximum keeps a localised heavy spell visible in a large district; it reads closer to the heaviest station report than to the district average.
  • Values are raw model rainfall in mm; no bias correction.

MME mean, median and chance

  • MME (mean): simple average of the models' district values, all with equal weight.
  • MME median: the middle value of the models (for an even number, the mean of the two middle values); a single very wet or very dry model cannot pull it.
  • Chance ≥ category: share of the models whose district value reaches the category's lower limit (e.g. 7 of 10 models ≥ 64.5 mm = 70%). Not calibrated against observations.
  • On days a model does not reach (ICON after Day 7, ARPEGE after Day 4, IMD GFS after Day 5, NCUM-R after Day 3), the MME uses the remaining models; the tooltip shows how many.

Late models

Every run also stores its rainfall for the next run's day windows (shifted by 12 h). If a model's run for the current IC is not yet out, the page uses these values from its previous run, for exactly the same 24 hours, includes them in the MME and labels the model e.g. “ECMWF IFS (12Z)”. They are replaced automatically when the new run arrives.

Updates and archive

Checked every 30 minutes, 08:40–16:10 and 20:40–04:10 IST, plus the six main updates; each model is added as soon as it is published. Last 7 days (14 runs) kept.

  • Each member's district value is computed exactly as in section 7 (highest grid-point rainfall in the district).
  • Pooling: the selected ensembles are pooled member by member; every member counts equally. With all three on, ECMWF weighs 49%, GEFS 30% and GEPS 21%; the pooled chance is the member-weighted average of the three chances.
  • Chance ≥ category = members at or above the category's lower limit ÷ members selected. Raw ensemble frequency, not calibrated.
  • Members' rainfall: Min, 10th, 25th, 50th (median), 75th, 90th percentile and Max of the members' district values (linear interpolation between members), and the ensemble mean.
  • Example, Guntur, Day 1, 00 UTC 03 Nov, ≥ 64.5 mm: ECMWF 50/50, GEFS 26/31, GEPS 18/21 → 94/102 = 92%.
  • An ensemble not yet published for a run is shown as “(not yet)” and added when it arrives; runs are never mixed. Last 7 days kept.

9. Subdivisional Forecast: rainfall, Tmax, Tmin ↑ top

Three tabs, Rainfall, Maximum Temperature and Minimum Temperature, for the 36 IMD meteorological subdivisions. Each tab has a map coloured by the MME and a table of all subdivisions with the MME and every model. Hover over or tap a subdivision on the map to see each model's value; the table can be downloaded as CSV and the map as PNG. Direct links: #sub-rain, #sub-tmax, #sub-tmin.

Subdivision value

  • The grid points of a subdivision are those of its districts (as in section 7: points inside each district plus the one nearest its centre). Subdivision of each district as in IMD's map.
  • Rainfall: the highest 24-h rainfall at any grid point in the subdivision, for each model and Day 1–10. It is taken from the same district values as the MME District Rainfall tab (the highest of the subdivision's districts), so the ten models and the day windows are the same.
  • Maximum temperature of a date: the highest 2-m temperature at any grid point in the subdivision during 00–12 UTC (05:30–17:30 IST).
  • Minimum temperature of a date: the lowest 2-m temperature at any grid point in the subdivision from 12 UTC of the previous day to 03 UTC (17:30–08:30 IST), IMD's minimum-temperature period.
  • MME: simple mean of these model values, equal weight, using the models that reach the day.
  • Temperature dates: a 00 UTC run gives Tmax from the IC date and Tmin from the next date; a 12 UTC run gives both from the next date. Values are raw model output in °C; no bias or height correction.

  • IMD GFS, NCUM-G and NCUM-R are in the Rainfall tab only: IMD's server folder for this site holds their rainfall files, not temperature.
  • IFS, GFS and ICON give the true maximum and minimum. The others give temperature at fixed hours, so their maximum can be 1–2 °C low (most for AIFS and AIGFS, every 6 h).
  • Hill subdivisions: the lowest value comes from the highest grid points, so the minimum for Jammu & Kashmir–Ladakh, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Arunachal Pradesh and Sub-Himalayan West Bengal & Sikkim is far below the station minima (often −15 to −30 °C). Use these subdivisions' Tmin for trends between days and models, not as station values.

Updates and archive

Temperature is checked every 30 minutes with the district rainfall; each model is added as soon as its run is out. Rainfall follows the district rainfall files. Last 7 days (14 runs) kept.

10. Verification ↑ top

Observed rainfall: two sources

  • IMD Gridded: IMD real-time gridded daily rainfall at 0.25° (IMD Pune), downloaded openly each day; provisional, so the last three days are re-fetched as IMD revises them. The district value is the maximum or the average of the grid points inside the district.
  • IMD DRMS: the gauge-based district-average rainfall from IMD's iRAINS District Rainfall Monitoring Scheme, via its district API (matched by district code, so matching is exact).
  • The rainfall of a date is the 24 h ending 08:30 IST (03 UTC) of the next day, the IMD convention.
  • The Verification tab has two dropdowns: Observed (IMD Gridded or IMD DRMS) and Forecast value (district maximum or district average). DRMS, being a district average, is compared with the forecast district average.

Forecasts compared

  • Each model's forecast is matched to the observation by district code and cut to the same 08:30–08:30 IST window; the forecast district value is the maximum or the average of the grid points, to match the chosen observed value. For 6-hourly models (ECMWF AIFS, NOAA AIGFS) the totals at 03/15 UTC are interpolated in time.
  • Day 1: 12 UTC run of the day before (the run available at 08:30 IST) or 00 UTC run of the same day; Day 2 and Day 3 from runs one and two days earlier.
  • MME mean and median are formed from the models available for that window.

Districts without observed data are left out.

Permanent archive: monthly and seasonal verification

  • Every observed day is stored for good in an archive (a separate branch, verif-archive, of the site's repository, also copied to the site as verif/archive/). Each day's file holds the observed district values and every model's district values for the six forecasts of that day (Day 1, 2 and 3 from the 12 UTC and the 00 UTC runs).
  • On the Verification tab, Scores over adds up the scores for: the selected day, the last 7 or 30 days, any month, any IMD season (winter Jan–Feb, pre-monsoon Mar–May, monsoon Jun–Sep, post-monsoon Oct–Dec), or the whole archive.
  • Download all daily scores (CSV) gives one row per day, forecast (Day and run), model or MME, and threshold, with hits, misses, false alarms and correct negatives, ready for a spreadsheet: add up the counts over any period and compute POD, FAR, CSI and bias from the totals.
  • The archive begins with the observed day 01 Nov 2026; forecasts are in it from the 00 UTC 02 Nov run onwards.

12. Data sources and acknowledgements ↑ top

  • IMD: CLIMS RS/RW data; NWP Division SFTP server (IMD GFS, NCUM-G and NCUM-R fields and rainfall, cut to the region); IMD Pune real-time gridded rainfall; iRAINS District Rainfall Monitoring Scheme API (gauge-based district rainfall).
  • ECMWF open data (CC BY 4.0): IFS, AIFS, ENS rainfall, IFS and AIFS 2-m temperature, and tropical-cyclone tracks.
  • NOAA/NCEP: GFS, AIGFS and GEFS rainfall; GFS and AIGFS 2-m temperature (NOAA Open Data Dissemination on AWS; NOMADS).
  • Environment and Climate Change Canada: GDPS and GEPS (MSC Datamart, open data licence).
  • DWD (ICON) and Météo-France (ARPEGE) open data.
  • JTWC / UCAR RAL: open a-deck model tracks.
  • District boundaries: IMD DRMS district map (742 districts, with IMD district codes); IMD meteorological subdivisions (36).
  • Computing and hosting: GitHub Actions and GitHub Pages (free).
